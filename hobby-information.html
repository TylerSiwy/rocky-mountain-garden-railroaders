G Gauge / G scale Model Railroading

G scale was first introduced during the 1930’s. Layouts were set up either in the garden or the attic because of the size. The scale disappeared during the Second World War and post war attention was turned toward the smaller HO and N scale trains.

Then in 1968, a German manufacturer reintroduced G Gauge by modelling a European narrow gauge train set on a 45 mm wide track at a scale ratio of 1:22.6. As the scale’s popularity increased, more types of locomotives and rolling stock were added. Some narrow North American models were also offered, always maintaining the 1:22.6 ratios.

By the mid 1980’s G Gauge was firmly established and several manufacturers were offering a broad range of rolling stock and locomotive products for sale (though scale ratios normally vary between 1:20.3 to 1:32; depending upon the manufacturer). Other companies began to produce accessories such as buildings, people and other track-side items to complement the layouts being built by G Gauge / G scale enthusiasts.

A number of those enthusiastic modellers who were modelling in “G”, who scratch built items for their own layouts began to mass-produce these items for sale to hobby shops and their fellow modellers.

 
Magazines such as Garden Railways were started with the goal of furthering the popularity of the scale through feature articles on outdoor layouts, product reviews of G Gauge / G scale trains and accessories. Videos about garden railroads using “G “ have been produced. These are available through various sources.

Today, G Gauge / G scale continues to increase in popularity. Many modellers like its size, which is “big enough to get one’s hands on”, while still being able to turn in a 4 foot diameter. The trains are weather proof and the track can be left outdoors all year around. When used in a garden setting, the combination of soil, plants, rock and changing sunlight together with such options as smoke puffing engines and a sound system recreating a steam or diesel sound, bells, whistles and horns, combine to make the G Gauge / G scale experience one of incredible realism and personal satisfaction.

A layout indoors is also a great source of relaxation for the whole family. Of course, a train running around the tree at Christmas time can bring back fond child hood memories for many of us.

The National Model Railroaders Association (NMRA) www.nmra.org is currently setting up standards to meet the variations in scale & gauge for garden railroading along with the Model Railroad Industry Association.
